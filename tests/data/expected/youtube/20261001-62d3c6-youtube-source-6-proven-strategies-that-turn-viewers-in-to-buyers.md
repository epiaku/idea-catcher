**6 Proven Strategies That Turn Viewers In To Buyers** by _Ed Lawrence_

## 📝 Summary

Ed Lawrence explains six ways to turn YouTube viewers into buyers: buy-now, call, waitlist, DM, webinar, and ascension funnels. He compares the trust each must build, the work involved, and when to choose each one (0:23, 16:18).

## 🎯 Main Purpose

Choose the simplest funnel that builds enough trust for your offer’s price and your audience’s preferences. A direct purchase link may be enough for a low-cost product, while expensive offers often need more conversation or education; more complex funnels should come after an offer and sales process have proved themselves (0:23, 16:18).

## 💡 Key Examples

- Buy-now: videos link directly to an offer. Lawrence says this works best for offers under about $250, including software and digital products (1:29, 2:05).
- Call: viewers visit a sales page, complete a short application, and book a call. Lawrence describes reviewing applications manually rather than automatically rejecting people who may have rushed through a form (3:01, 4:29, 5:05).
- Waitlist: Lawrence describes opening 50 places for a limited sales window, then returning to a waitlist. In his example, roughly 10% of people on the list became clients when sales opened (6:53, 7:17, 7:41).
- DM: a viewer sends a keyword through Instagram or starts a WhatsApp conversation, receives information, and can ask follow-up questions asynchronously (9:18, 9:29).
- Webinar: Lawrence says sales from one event were slow during the live session but stronger after email follow-up (11:38, 11:50).
- Ascension: a buyer starts with a low-cost offer and is introduced to higher-priced offers after getting value from each purchase (14:36, 15:00).

## ✅ Action Plan

- Set the offer and price, then consider how much trust a viewer needs before buying (0:23).
- If the offer is inexpensive and the channel is new, start by linking videos directly to a clear sales page (1:29, 16:18).
- If the offer is expensive, consider an application and call; review applicants, send attendance reminders, and follow up after calls (5:05, 5:41, 5:53).
- Use a waitlist only when availability or enrollment windows are genuinely limited; keep providing useful content while people wait (8:16, 8:39).
- Consider DMs if buyers prefer messaging, or a webinar if there is an audience and email list to invite and follow up with (10:22, 12:10, 16:30).
- Build an ascension funnel only after an offer and sales system are proven, and make the path between purchases clear (15:31, 16:01, 16:43).
- Check that the offer on the landing page matches what viewers came from the video wanting to buy (16:48).

## 💬 Tips

| Tip | Explanation | How to Apply |
| --- | ----------- | ------------ |
| Do not add steps without a reason. | For some offers, videos and a purchase link provide enough trust; extra automation can complicate the journey (1:13, 1:29). | Test a direct link to a relevant offer before building a more elaborate funnel (1:29). |
| Start call qualification simply. | A rigid form can reject a suitable buyer who answers quickly or inaccurately (4:29, 4:55). | Ask a few questions, allow booking, then review the responses before the call (5:05). |
| Plan the follow-up. | Lawrence says some call sales happen afterward, and his webinar example depended heavily on post-event emails (5:53, 11:38). | Prepare reminders and relevant follow-up messages before inviting people to book or register (6:17, 12:34). |
| Make each purchase earn the next one. | An ascension funnel relies on buyers getting value from an initial offer; scattered offers create confusion (15:00, 15:31). | Prove one offer works before introducing a clearly connected next offer (16:01, 16:43). |

- Make the offer on each landing page closely match the problem its video addresses—for example, link a demo about capturing ideas to a relevant Idea Catcher offer if that offer is available (16:48).
